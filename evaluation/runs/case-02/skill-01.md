# Case 02 — Skill (B)

## Model Output

### 1. Decision State

**Facts:**
- A pays 40% more than B
- A is 996, big stable company, same role scope
- B has lower pay but broader tech stack exposure, normal WLB, smaller growing company
- User deliberating for days

**Assumptions:**
- Mid-career, not entry-level
- "Growth opportunity" at B means new domains/ownership
- The 40% delta is meaningful to current lifestyle

**Unknowns (critical):**
- What is actual financial baseline? Is delta "nice to have" or "life-changing"?
- Career horizon? Building toward specific role in 3-5 years or optimizing next 12 months?
- What does "small but growing" mean concretely? Headcount, funding, market position?

**Stakeholders:**
- Current self and future self
- A's team (will consume waking hours)
- B's team (betting on company trajectory)

### 2. Value of Information

Three high-VOI questions:

1. **If you took B and the company failed in 18 months, would you still be glad you did it for the skills you built?** (tests whether growth is the real goal)
2. **At A, what actual decision authority or scope expansion happens after 12 months of 996?** (tests whether A is a treadmill)
3. **Does the 40% delta change what you can say no to?** (tests whether money is strategic or consumption)

### 3. Strategy Evaluation

**Offer A: High pay, 996, same role**

- Fragility: **HIGH** — no margin for health issue, bad manager, or reorg
- Exposure Surface: Reorg, burnout spiral, layoffs
- Recovery Cost: Exit with same skills + fatigue. Interview from depletion.
- Reversibility: **LOW** — once in 996, exit path narrows
- Asymmetry: Gains capped (salary). Losses uncapped (health, relationships, career trajectory, missed skill compounding). **Negative asymmetry.**
- Narrative Debt: "I took the money" — no equity in that story

**Offer B: Lower pay, broader stack, WLB**

- Fragility: **MEDIUM** — company risk is real, but skills are portable
- Exposure Surface: Company runs out of money, "broad stack" = chaotic firefighting, growth stalls
- Recovery Cost: If B fails, interview with broader skill set. Financial hit is bounded.
- Reversibility: **HIGH** — WLB means always can interview. Door to big company stays open.
- Asymmetry: Gains uncapped (new skills compound across career). Losses capped (salary delta). **Positive asymmetry.**
- Narrative Debt: "I bet on growth" — story compounds even if it doesn't work

### 4. Event Chain Simulation

**Branch A (take the money):**
Node 1 (Month 1-3): Honeymoon. Paycheck feels good. 996 sustainable because new.
Node 2 (Month 4-8): Grind sets in. Same work. No new skills. Peers learning things you're not.
Node 3 (Month 9-12): Decision point. Double down internally or realize trapped.
Node 4 (Month 12-18): Either made it work or exiting depleted with same resume + a logo.

**Branch B (take the growth):**
Node 1 (Month 1-6): Steep learning curve. WLB means actually processing. Confidence expanding.
Node 2 (Month 6-12): Skills compounding. Can speak to architecture you couldn't before.
Node 3 (Month 12-18): Either company growing with you, or you exit with resume that opens doors at both big cos and startups.

**The branch that changes the conclusion:** If B is genuinely unstable and 40% from A lets you save a year of expenses in 6 months → A becomes a calculated extraction mission.

### 5. Stakeholder Simulation

- **Current self:** Wants both money and growth. Doesn't know which version of self in 18 months.
- **Future self (18 months):** Will judge by: did this expand or contract options?
- **A's hiring manager:** Wants output. Exhaustion is retention mechanism. No need to offer growth.
- **B's hiring manager:** Needs you to level up. Incentives partially aligned.

### 6. Recommendation

**Take B.** Not because safer — because of asymmetry: B's downside bounded, upside unbounded (skill compounding). A has the opposite.

**What would change this:**
- If 40% delta = precarity → safety net, A as 12-month extraction with hard exit date
- If B's "broader stack" is just "understaffed chaos" with no mentorship

**Commitment Rule:** At 6 months, ask "Am I more or less employable than 6 months ago?" If less or same → exit.

**Concrete next step:** Go back to B's hiring manager: "Walk me through my first 6 months. What specific technologies or domains will I own that I don't own today?" Concrete answer = confirmation. Vague = red flag.
